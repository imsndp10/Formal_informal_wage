\begin{abstract}	
\noindent 
This study investigates wage disparities between formal and informal employment, assessing whether these differences stem from labor market segmentation or voluntary choices. Using data from the Nepal Labor Force Survey rounds II (2008) and III (2018), we apply unconditional quantile regression and RIF-OLS decomposition to examine wage differentials across various occupational skill levels. Our findings reveal significant wage disparities that vary across occupations and quantiles. In 2008, high-skilled workers exhibited increasing wage disparities across quantiles, while low-skilled workers showed a more stable pattern. By 2018, wage disparities for high-skilled roles increased up to the median, whereas they decreased for low-skilled workers, indicating a convergence in wage structures over the decade. Although wage gaps in informal employment have decreased over time, they remain significant at the median, indicating that informal employment offers both structural barriers and voluntary benefits. These findings highlight the need for policies addressing both the persistent inequalities and the duality of informal employment.

\end{abstract}
